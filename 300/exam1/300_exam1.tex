\question[4]
Write a concise definition of evolution.

\AnswerBox{0.1\textheight}{%
Genetic change (1 pt) in a population (1 pt) over time (1 pt).
}

\question[2]

\begin{choices}
\choice Natural selection acts on populations, not on individuals.
\choice Evolutionary change occurs at the level of the individual.
\correctchoice Mutations are the raw material for natural selection.
\choice Developmental change is a population-level process.
\choice Species are simply those organisms that share similar phenotypes.
\end{choices}

\begin{choices}

\question[2]
Gene trees can vary for different loci. What does this imply for species trees?

\begin{choices}
\choice Accurately estimating a gene tree from the longest gene will always give the correct species phylogeny.

\question[2]
Which of these describes paralogous genes?

\begin{choices}
\correctchoice The result of a gene duplication within a species; for example, a copy of the original hemoglobin gene that evolved a slightly different functions within a mammal species as hemoglobin~α and~β.
\choice The result of a speciation event; each derived species has evolved a different version of the gene, like the difference between hemoglobin α in humans and chimps.
\choice Genes located on the same chromosome and that are genetically linked, like freckles and hair color in humans.
\choice Genes that have independently evolved the same function in distantly related organisms, like the hearing gene, Prestin, which allows echolocation in both dolphins and bats.
\choice Genes that have changed within a species as a result of adaptive evolution, such as improvements in the defensins proteins of the human immune system over time.
\end{choices}

\question[5]
Explain why Darwin's idea of natural selection was not widely accepted until after the Evolutionary Synthesis.

\AnswerBox{0.05\textheight}{Lacked suitable genetic mechanism until E.S. rediscovered Mendel's work.}


\question[5]
Assume a locus with three alleles in equilibrium. Given $p^2=0.04$  and $q^2 = 0.01,$ calculate the frequency of the third allele, $r.$ Show your work for full credit!

\AnswerBox{0.15\textheight}{%
\begin{align*}
p &= \sqrt{0.04} = 0.2 \\
q &= \sqrt{0.01} = 0.1 \\
r &= 1 - 0.2 - 0.1 = \mathbf{0.7}
\end{align*}}%$$r = 0.7$$}

\question[5]
Explain the difference between paralogous and orthologous genes.

\AnswerBox{0.1\textheight}{Paralogs arise via duplication. Orthologs arise via speciation.}

\question[5]
%The Asian Honey Bee \textit{(Apis cerana)} has a genome size of about $2.3 \times 10^{8}$ base pairs. The mutation rate is about $3.4 \times 10^{-9}.$ About how many new mutations would you expect in every gamete?
\textit{Caenorhabditis elegans} (a nematode) has a genome size of approximately $1 \times 10^8$ base pairs. It's mutation rate is about $2.1 \times 10^{-8}$ per bp. About how many new mutations would you expect in every gamete?

\AnswerBox{0.1\textheight}{2.1}

\question[5]
Explain why non-synonymous substitutions might be subject to natural selection but synonymous substitutions are not.

\AnswerBox{0.1\textheight}{NS changes amino acid order and thus possibly protein function. S does not change amino acid so no protein function change.}

\question[5]
Explain the difference between subfunctionalization and neofunctionalization for duplicate genes.

\AnswerBox{0.1\textheight}{Sub: each copy does one part of the original function. Neo: one duplicate evolves new function; other retains original function.}
