\question[5]
Define turnover rate as it applies to the fossil record. %When turnover rate has been compared among multiple taxa, a distinct relationship has emerged between \emph{S} and \emph{O}. What is this relationship?

\AnswerBox{0.1\textheight}{Turnover rate is the rate at which new taxa replace old taxa in the fossil record. Taxa with low origination rates tend to have low extinction rates. Taxa with high O tend to have high S.}

Hellberg and Roy~(2001) studied populations of a marine snail north and south of Point Conception (see figure). They tested whether genetic differences were due to vicariance or range expansion. At right is a simplified version of their parsimony network.  Based on the network, state which hypothesis is supported and describe how the network supports the hypothesis you chose. 

Each oval represents one allele. Letter codes inside each oval is the sample site shown along the coast. Numbers in parentheses after each site is individuals with that allele. Each line connecting two ovals indicates one mutation difference between alleles.  Small circles indicate additional mutational differences between alleles.

\columnbreak

\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{exam3_hellberg_roy}

\end{multicols}


	\AnswerBox{0.1\textheight}{%
	Genetic diversity is much higher south of Point Conception compared to northern populations. Most individuals sampled from north of P.C. share the same allele, suggesting this region is much younger and therefore the result of recent range expansion.%
	}



\question[10]
Persimmon trees produce either male flowers (no carpels) or female flowers (no stamens). Use the floral quartet model to explain which (if any) class or classes of MADS genes could be lost (no expression) to produce 1) male flowers and 2) female flowers. If the model does not work for one or both sexes of flowers, explain why for each sex.

\AnswerBox{0.1\textheight}{%
Loss of Class B would produce female flowers. However, female flowers cannot be exlained by the loss of any one class without also losing male flowers. For example, Class C is needed for both male and female reproductive structures.

\question
\begin{multicols}{2}
Male treehoppers (genus \textit{Enchenopa}) attract females by sending vibrations through plants. One species sends low frequency vibrations through the stem of a plant. The other species sends high frequency vibrations through the petiole.  The frequency differences of the males maximize detection by their respective females in the same environment (McNett and Cocroft 2008).

\columnbreak

	\includegraphics[height=1.75in]{petiole_stem}
\end{multicols}

	\begin{parts}
	\part[5]
	Is this intrasexual or intersexual selection? Why?
	
	\AnswerBox{0.1\textheight}{%
	Intersexual. Females are choosing males based on a characteristic of the male.
	}
	
	\part[10]
	Did the frequency differences more likely evolved by sensory bias or sensory drive? Explain what is sensory bias or sensory drive (depending on your choice), and then explain how your choice is consistent with the scenario described above.

	\AnswerBox{0.3\textheight}{%
	Sensory drive. Sensory drive is when female drives evolution of a trait based on the ability of the trait to stand out from the environment. Here, the different vibrations standout relative to their environments.
	}
	\end{parts}

%\end{equation}
