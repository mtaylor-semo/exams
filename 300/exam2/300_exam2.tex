\question[5]
Explain why reproductive isolation is more likely to evolve between allopatric populations than within a sympatric population.

\AnswerBox{0.2\textheight}{%
	Allopatry isolates two populations, eliminating gene flow. This more easily allows for the evolution of reproductive isolation.  Gene flow can occur within a sympatric population, then making the evolution of reproductive isolation much more difficult.
}

\question[5]
Briefly explain the biological species concept. Briefly describe one problem with this concept for determining what is a species.

\AnswerBox{0.2\textheight}{%
Biological species concept states that individuals of the same species can interbreed with each other but not with individuals of other species. Requires sexual reproduction so does not apply to asexual species. Cannot be easily tested between allopatric species or across extant and extinct species.
}

\question[10]

\begin{multicols}{2}
\flushleft
The table at right (Sharp et al.~2005) shows how \textit{E. coli} uses 32 codons from 40 highly expressed genes. The tabled numbers are the ratio of actual to expected use if all codons were used equally. A ratio of 1 means the codon is used exactly as often as expected (e.g., Met, in bold). Explain whether the data provide evidence for natural selection at the genetic level. Explain why or why not. Use 1--2 specific codons from the table to support your answer.
\columnbreak
{\addfontfeatures{Numbers=Lining}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llrcllr@{}}
	\toprule
	\multicolumn{7}{l}{Codon use in \textit{E. coli}.}\\
	\midrule
	Phe & UUU & 0.45 &  & Ser & UCU & 2.54\\
Phe & UUC & 1.55 &  & Ser & UCC & 1.52\\
Leu & UUA & 0.14 &  & Ser & UCA & 0.19\\
Leu & UUG & 0.25 &  & Ser & UCG & 0.06\\
Leu & CUU & 0.30 &  & Pro & CCU & 0.59\\
Leu & CUC & 0.23 &  & Pro & CCC & 0.05\\
Leu & CUA & 0.01 &  & Pro & CCA & 0.52\\
Leu & CUG & 5.07 &  & Pro & CCG & 2.85\\
Ile & AUU & 0.72 &  & Thr & ACU & 1.89\\
Ile & AUC & 2.27 &  & Thr & ACC & 1.75\\
Ile & AUA & 0.01 &  & Thr & ACA & 0.19\\
\textbf{Met} & \textbf{AUG} & \textbf{1.00} &  & Thr & ACG & 0.17\\
Val & GUU & 2.07 &  & Ala & GCU & 1.83\\
Val & GUC & 0.30 &  & Ala & GCC & 0.32\\
Val & GUA & 1.14 &  & Ala & GCA & 1.09\\
Val & GUG & 0.48 &  & Ala & GCG & 0.76\\
	\bottomrule
	\end{tabular}}
\end{multicols}
%	\end{minipage}
	\begin{solution}
	The data do provide evidence for natural selection. Methionine is 1 because AUG is the only possible codon. Codons like AUC (2.27) and UCU (2.54) are used 2X more than expected. CUG is used 5X more than expected. When some codons are used more than random (codon bias), then other codons have to be used less often than random (values below 1). That provides evidence of natural selection because using few codons more often increases translation efficiency.
	\end{solution}
