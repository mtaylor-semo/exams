\question[10]
Explain “turnover rate” on islands. Explain how the target effect and the rescue effect alters the turnover rate on islands of different sizes and degrees of isolation, as appropriate.

\AnswerBox{0.3\textheight}{%
	Turnover rate is replacement of extinct species with new, different species. Rescue effect lowers turnover rate because high immigration to near islands lowers extinction. Larger islands have higher turnover rate as immigration is easier on large islands, so new species can replace extinct species more quicky.
}

\question[5]
Explain the “island rule” and how \textit{Homo floresiensis} fits the rule. 

\AnswerBox{0.2\textheight}{%
	Island rule suggests that small vertebrates can become larger on islands (perhaps due to release from predators) and large vertebrates become smaller on islands (perhaps due to more resource limitation). The small \textit{Homo floresiensis} evolved from larger \textit{H.~erectus.}
}

\question[10]
Suppose the robin is endangered. Describe how you can use information from the figure to increase the abundance of this species in the more isolated fragments (e.g., 3,000–6,000 km). Your answer must depend on the natural dispersal ability of the robin, which you can infer from the figure. You may not introduce the robin to any habitat fragment. You may not kill other organisms that might be present. That goes against your conservation principles.

\AnswerBox{0.2\textheight}{%
	The robin has a relatively low dispersal ability, as it is found most often in fragments much less than 1000 m. It also needs freagments at least 10 ha or larger.  Thus, need to create stepping stones (or corridor) at least 10 ha in size that are within 100-200 m (generally) of each other.
}

\question[5]
Describe nestedness on islands. Explain how filters can create a pattern of nestedness.

\AnswerBox{0.1\textheight}{%
	The species present on distant islands are subsets of species present on closer islands. Filters limit dispersal ability of some species, so filters remove species that can not disperse to the farther islands.
}

\question[10]
\textit{Homo neanderthalensis} lived across Europe for at least 100,000 years during the Pleistocene. Recent evidence suggests that Neanderthals went extinct due to climate change. Explain how the mild climate around the Mediterranean Sea may have allowed Neandertals to survive longer across \textit{all} of Europe during the late Pleistocene. 

\AnswerBox{0.2\textheight}{%
	The climate acted as refugia during glacial advances so pockets of Neandertals survived in that region. They were then able to expand back into Europe as the glaciers retreated. Eventually, they went extinct as population sizes reduced.
}

\question[4]
Briefly summarize the Replacement model as it relates to the evolution and range expansion of \textit{Homo.}

\AnswerBox{0.1\textheight}{All species of \textit{Homo} first evolved in Africa. \textit{H.~erectus} left first and expanded across Europe and Asia. \textit{H.~sapiens} evolved later. As it expanded across the globe it replaced \textit{H.~erectus}.}
