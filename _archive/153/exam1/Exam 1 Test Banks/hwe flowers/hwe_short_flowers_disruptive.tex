\question[5]
The flower population (from the previous page) is not in Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium because the sample contains an excess of both homozygotes and very few of the heterozygotes, compared to expected frequencies (not shown).  You suspect natural selection might explain the differences between observed and expected frequencies.  Which mode of selection would explain the excess of both homozygotes and relatively few heterozygotes?  Explain why. 

\begin{solution}
Disruptive selection (2 points). 

Have more of both homozygotes and a deficit heterozygotes, suggesting the heterozygotes are beings selected against / homozygotes being selected for. (3 points).

\end{solution}
