\question[5]
The flower population (from the previous page) is not in Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium because the sample contains an excess of one homozygote and very few of the other homozygotes, compared to expected frequencies (not shown).  You suspect natural selection might explain the differences between observed and expected frequencies.  Which mode of selection would explain excess of one particular homozygote?  Explain why. 

\begin{solution}
Directional selection (2 points). 

Have more of one homozygote, a moderate excess of heterozygotes and few of the other homozygote, suggesting one extreme phenotypes are is beings selected against / for. (3 points).

\end{solution}
